Hello all,

Long version below.

For a concrete application, I would like to generate tolerance and
prediction regions of straight lines obtained by mixed-effects linear
regression, with random slope and intercept, and including this
randoms variations also.

Is there any package that allows to do this, like a

md <- lm( Y ~ X, data = d )
predict( md, newdata = dp, interval = "pred" )

for a classical linear model?

** Long version **

The context is to determine limits of blank (LOB), detection (LOD) and
quantification (LOQ) of an analytical method that uses a calibration
curve, Y = a X + b where X is the concentration and Y the observed
value. First step is to find a and b from known (X, Y) values, and
then to determine unknown values X* of X using observed Y values,
hence X* = (Y - b)/a. Of course, X* != X even for training values.

The method is used with different reagents, leading to a
(potential) fixed effect on a and b, and in different centers, leading
to a (potential) random effect on a and b also. Limits (LOB, LOD, LOQ)
should be defined for each reagent, but for an average center, taking
into account between-center variability.

Fitting the model with lmer works quite well, and Gaussian assumptions
are reasonnably met.

LOB is the 0,95 quantile of the X* distribution when X equals 0, which
is one condition available of the data --- so it is the upper limit of
a 95% prediction, or eventually tolerance, unilateral interval.
Currently, I reconstruct it ? by hand ? adapting the basic formula:

IP = m + t * s * sqrt( 1 + 1/n )

with m = average, s = standard deviation, t quantile of a Student
distribution with n-1 ddl and n the sample size. Adaptation is to
define s? as the sum of residual variance and between-center variance,
and usethe Kendall-Rogers approximation of the dl.

Is there anything better than that?

LOD is defined as the lowest X for which the 0.05 quantile of X*
distribution equals the LOB. Hence, it may be obtained by finding the
intersection of the vertical line ? X = LOB ? and the boundary of the
90% prediction interval of the straight line [upper if a > 0, lower if
a < 0].  Hence my question on how to obtain the prediction region of
the straight line, for a given reagent but taking into account both
between-center and residual variability?

Eventually, would ? forgetting ? the random effect and using a
classical linear model, for which predict() works, give a reasonnable
value, or problems of correlations between values would make this
highly unreliable?

Thanks in advance for any help,

Hello,

I have some skewed, nonnegative longitudinal data, and following initial model exploration I decided to model it with a generalized linear mixed model, specifically a mixed-effects Gamma regression with a log link. However, I was surprised to see some differences in fit statistics for a glmer fit vs one with glmmTMB.

I have now tried this with a publicly available dataset in order to produce a minimal working example. See below for an example that uses the epil2 dataset (comes with glmmTMB):

library(glmmTMB)
library(lme4)
data(epil2)
epil2_red <- filter(epil2, y>0) # gamma requires y>0
mod_glmer    <- glmer(y ~ trt + (1 | subject), data = epil2_red, family = Gamma(link = "log"))
mod_glmmTMB  <- glmmTMB(y ~ trt + (1 | subject), data = epil2_red, family = Gamma(link = "log"))

AIC(mod_glmer, mod_glmmTMB)
summary(mod_glmer)
summary(mod_glmmTMB)

The fixed effects estimates are not wildly different, but more so than I had expected. The variance components look quite different to my eye, as well as AIC.

When I use the two packages to fit Poisson GLMMs with the same effects to the same dataset, the resulting models do not differ appreciably. The AIC values are identical, as is the variance estimate for the random intercept.

Further, possibly off-topic details: I have also fit this model in SAS using PROC GLIMMIX twice, once using Laplace and once using Gauss-Hermite quadrature for the likelihood approximation, following a suggestion that perhaps different approximation methods could explain the discrepancy. However, the two SAS results were trivially different from each other, very similar to the results of glmmTMB, and less similar to the results of glmer. This makes me think that either I am misunderstanding how to specify Gamma (but not Poisson) models in glmer, or else something is not correct with the glmer fit.

all factors. In the list of conditions below, they are labeled V, F, D, and
their values are V1 versus V2, etc. The fixed-effect structure thus
intersects with the random-effects structure as displayed below. The
notation X1 vs X2 before the semi-colon indicates that that contrast
between values was tested in that condition, while the notation Y1 or Y2,
Z1 or Z2 after the semi-colon indicates that those values of those
contrasts were held constant in that condition. I treated the random
effects as uncorrelated.

Condition number is listed first, followed by the contrast for that
condition, followed by the values of the other contrasts that were held
constant in that condition. Across the 12 conditions, all possible
contrasts are tested with all possible combinations of constant values for
the other contrasts. To make this even more explicit, a participant in
condition 1 was exposed to 2 sets of stimuli, one exemplifying V1, the
other V2, and within both V1 and V2, F and D were held constant at values
F1 and D1. A participant in condition 4 was also exposed to stimuli from V1
and V2, but F and D were held constant at F2 and D2. Participants in
condition 9 and 12 were also exposed to stimuli from V1 and V2 with F and D
held constant at one or the other of their possible values. The other
conditions fill out the remaining combinations of contrasts and constants.

1 V1 vs V2; F1, D1

2 D1 vs D2; V1, F1

3 F1 vs F2; V1, D1

4 V1 vs V2; F2, D2

5 F1 vs F2; V1, D2

6 D1 vs D2; V1, F2

7 F1 vs F2; V2, D1

8 D1 vs D2; V2, F2

9 V1 vs V2; F2, D1

10 D1 vs D2; V2, F1

11 F1 vs F2; V2, D2

12 V1 vs V2; F1, D2

Dear John,

Please keep the mailing list in cc. Others might chime in or benefit from
this as well.

You should add the three way interaction of the design as fixed effects.
Then the condition random effect is a confounder with the fixed effect. It
should have a near zero variance should you add it anyway.

IMHO you want something like this

response ~ V * F * D + (1|participant) + (1|stimulus) +
(1|participant:stimulus)

Dear all,
Do you work with experimental or observational data and want to take your statistical modelling skills beyond standard linear and generalized linear models?
 
We have the last seats for our 4-day online course on Generalized Linear Mixed Models (GLMMs) in R, taking place 21?24 September.
 
Course website: [ https://www.physalia-courses.org/courses-workshops/glmms-in-r/ ]( https://www.physalia-courses.org/courses-workshops/glmms-in-r/ ) 
 
 
This course provides a practical introduction to specifying, interpreting, and validating mixed models in R, with a focus on the lme4 and glmmTMB packages.
 
During the course, participants will explore:
- Regression fundamentals, including interactions, contrasts, scaling, centering, and ANOVA
- Random effects and variance structures for grouped and heterogeneous data
- Model specification, selection, and interpretation for GLMMs
- Spatial, temporal, and phylogenetic correlation structures
- Model diagnostics, visualization, and assumption checking using effects and DHARMa
- Practical approaches to analysing experimental and observational data in R
 
The course combines lectures, discussions, Q&A sessions, and practical exercises using Slack and Zoom, with plenty of opportunities to work through applied examples and ask questions.
Whether you are already using mixed models or are looking to build a solid foundation in GLMMs, this course will provide practical tools and approaches that you can apply directly to your own data.
